Step 1 of 6

Encounter · Notice the Difference

Discuss the situations before studying the rules.

SITUATION A · REGULAR

“I usually work from home.”

A normal part of my routine.

SITUATION B · TEMPORARY

“This week, I’m working at the office.”

Something different for a limited period.

Discuss with a partner

  1. Which situation describes a regular routine?
  2. Which one is temporary?
  3. What changes in the verb forms?
Simple Present describes the regular routine. Present Progressive describes a temporary situation happening around now.
Step 2 of 6

Clarify · Understand the Grammar

Discover how meaning changes the tense, then compare sentence patterns.

1 · Meaning and use

Simple Present

Habits, routines, and general facts.

She speaks English. / I work five days a week.

Present Progressive

Actions happening now and temporary situations.

She is speaking now. / I’m working extra hours this week.

2 · Sentence patterns

FormSimple PresentPresent Progressive
AffirmativeShe works every day.She is working now.
NegativeShe doesn’t work on Sundays.She isn’t working today.
QuestionDoes she work from home?Is she working today?

Remember: He/she/it takes -s or -es in Simple Present affirmative sentences. After doesn’t, use the base verb. Present Progressive uses am/is/are + verb-ing.

Spelling: make → making; run → running; study → studying.

3 · Time expressions

Simple Present: usually, always, often, every day, on Mondays.

Present Progressive: right now, at the moment, currently, this week, these days.

Time expressions are clues; context and meaning decide the tense.

4 · Spot the mistake

She work every day. · I am work right now. · Does she works here? · I working today. · I’m knowing the answer.

She works every day. · I am working right now. · Does she work here? · I am working today. · I know the answer.

Know, like, want, and need are generally not used in progressive forms when describing states.

Step 4 of 6

Internalize · My Usual Life vs. My Life Right Now

Partner interview: answer in complete sentences, add details, and ask follow-up questions.

Part A · Regular habits

  1. What time do you usually wake up?
  2. How do you normally spend your weekends?
  3. How often do you practice English?
  4. What do you usually do when you have free time?

Part B · Current or temporary activities

  1. What are you learning these days?
  2. Are you working on any personal goals right now?
  3. Are you doing anything differently this month?
  4. What are you currently doing to improve your English?

Speaking support

Sentence starters: I usually… · At the moment, I’m… · These days, I’m trying to… · Normally I…, but this week I’m…

Follow-ups: Why? · How often? · What about you? · How is that going?

Suggested timing: 2 minutes preparation, 10 minutes exchanging answers, 3 minutes sharing or feedback.

Step 5 of 6

Fluent Use · Real-World Communication

Choose a scenario. Prepare keywords, not a full script, then switch roles.

Scenario A · Catching Up With a Friend

You haven’t seen your friend for months. Talk about your usual routines and what’s different these days.

  • What do you usually do during the week?
  • Are you studying or working on anything new?
  • Is anything changing in your routine these days?
  • How are you spending your free time lately?

Scenario B · Getting to Know a Candidate

You’re interviewing for a customer service role. Explain your routines, responsibilities, and skills you are developing.

  • What do you usually do during a typical weekday?
  • How do you normally organize your responsibilities?
  • Are you currently studying or developing any skills?
  • What are you doing to improve your communication skills?

Success challenge

Use at least two Simple Present sentences, two Present Progressive sentences, and two follow-up questions. Respond naturally rather than reading a script.
